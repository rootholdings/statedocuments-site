Forming an LLC or corporation: what to expect

You form an LLC or corporation by filing a short formation document with your state's business filing office and paying the state fee. Most states let you do it online.

What you usually need

  • A business name that's available in your state (most offices have a free name search).
  • A registered agent with a physical address in the state, to receive legal papers.
  • The formation document: often called Articles of Organization (LLC) or Articles of Incorporation (corporation). Some states use other names.
  • The state filing fee, paid to the state.

Things people miss

  • Forming the entity is a state filing. Tax IDs come separately. An EIN comes free from the IRS at irs.gov, and state tax registration is a different agency.
  • Many states require a recurring report and fee to stay active. See annual reports.
  • Private filing services exist and charge their own fees on top of the state fee. You can file directly with the state.

We are not a law firm. For help choosing an entity type, talk to a licensed attorney or accountant.
