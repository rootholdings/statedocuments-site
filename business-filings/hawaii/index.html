Business filings in Hawaii: LLC formation, annual reports and good standing

In Hawaii, LLCs and corporations are registered with the Hawaii Business Registration Division (DCCA). You file directly with that office and the fee goes to the state. Here is what to expect, with the fees we could confirm on official pages.

How to form an LLC in Hawaii, step by step

  1. Check that your business name is available. Most state filing offices, including the Hawaii Business Registration Division (DCCA), offer a free online name search.
  2. Pick a registered agent: a person or company with a physical street address in Hawaii who can accept legal papers for the business during business hours. You can serve as your own agent if you meet the state's rules.
  3. Prepare the formation document. For LLCs it is usually called Articles of Organization, though some states use another name (for example, Certificate of Formation or Certificate of Organization). Use the form or online filing on the official site.
  4. File it with the Hawaii Business Registration Division (DCCA) and pay the state fee. Online filing is usually the fastest option.
  5. Keep the filed document and receipt. Then get an EIN for free from the IRS at irs.gov and check whether you must register with Hawaii's tax agency, which is separate from the filing office.
  6. Put the next annual or periodic report on your calendar so the business stays in good standing.

Annual reports

Most states require a regular report (yearly or every two years) to keep a business active. Missing a report can lead the state to mark the business as not in good standing and eventually dissolve it.

Certificates of good standing

A certificate of good standing (sometimes called a certificate of existence or status) proves the business is registered and current. We could not confirm Hawaii's fee on an official page on November 13, 2026. Banks, lenders and other states often ask for one. Order it from the Hawaii Business Registration Division (DCCA).

Tips and common mistakes

  • Private filing services are optional. They charge their own fees on top of the state fee; you can always file directly with the state.
  • Be wary of official-looking letters from private companies offering to file your annual report or sell you a certificate for a large fee. Check the official site before paying.
  • Keep your registered agent and addresses up to date, or you may miss legal papers and state reminders.
