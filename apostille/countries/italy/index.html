Getting a U.S. document apostilled for Italy

General steps for sending a U.S. public document, such as a birth certificate, court record, or notarized document, to Italy. We are not affiliated with any government; the receiving authority and the official offices have the final word.

Apostille Convention status

Party to the Apostille Convention. Listed on the HCCH status table as Italy, by ratification on December 13, 1977; in force for Italy since February 11, 1978.

Source: HCCH status table, checked November 15, 2026 (table last updated June 30, 2026).

What to ask your state for

An apostille

Fees and processing times are set by each state office. See your state's page below or compare all states.

Which route applies

Because Italy is a party to the Apostille Convention, a U.S. public document generally needs an apostille from the office that covers the place where it was issued or notarized. For state and local documents, that is the state apostille office. For federal documents, it is the U.S. Department of State (travel.state.gov).

General steps

  1. Ask the person or authority in Italy that will receive the document exactly what they need, including whether they want an apostille, a translation, or a recently issued copy.
  2. Get the right original: a certified copy from the U.S. office that issued the document, or a document notarized by a notary commissioned in the state where you will request the apostille.
  3. Send it to the apostille office of the state that issued or notarized it (all 51 are listed below). Federal documents go to the U.S. Department of State instead.
  4. Follow that office's instructions for the request form, fee, and return delivery.
  5. When the apostille comes back, keep it attached to the document and send both to Italy.

These are general steps, not rules specific to Italy. We do not describe Italy's own requirements (translations, document age, local registration); ask the receiving authority.
