Getting an apostille in Illinois

In Illinois, apostilles for state and local documents are issued by the Illinois Secretary of State, Index Department. Below: the steps, the fee, how long it takes, and where to send your request, in plain English. The official page has the full, current instructions.

Official office

Illinois Secretary of State, Index Department

Open the official apostille page

www.ilsos.gov

Link checked November 13, 2026.

Fee

$2 per apostille or certificate. Each document needs its own.

As of November 13, 2026. Source: official page. Confirm before paying, since fees change.

Where and how to submit

Illinois Secretary of State, Index Department

Mail or in person (Chicago office, select DMV+ locations)

Full addresses, forms and hours: www.ilsos.gov

Processing time

Mail requests: 7–14 business days.

As of November 13, 2026. Source: official page. Times change with volume.

Step by step

  1. Get a certified copy from the right office: county clerk, local registrar or IDPH for birth records; county clerk for marriage records; the circuit clerk for divorce decrees.
  2. Fill out the request form from the Secretary of State Index Department page.
  3. By mail: send the form, documents, one check or money order payable to "Illinois Secretary of State," and a self-addressed stamped envelope to the Index Department, 160 N. LaSalle Street, Suite 100, Chicago.
  4. In person: bring the same items to the Chicago office (125 W. Monroe Street) or a listed DMV+ location such as Aurora.

Steps summarized in our own words from the official page on November 13, 2026. If anything differs, follow the official page.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Send one check for all documents instead of one per document.
  • Without prepaid postage, your documents will return by first-class mail.

What this office can apostille

Generally, documents issued by a Illinois public official (such as certified copies of vital records, court records, or state certificates) and documents notarized by a Illinois notary. Documents from other states go to that state. Federal documents, such as FBI background checks, go to the U.S. Department of State.

Before you start, ask whoever will receive the document abroad whether they need an apostille or a different certificate (countries outside the Hague Convention use authentication). Getting this wrong is the most common reason people have to start over.

Questions and answers

Who issues apostilles in Illinois?

The Illinois Secretary of State, Index Department issues apostilles for documents issued by Illinois public officials or notarized by a Illinois notary. Documents from other states go to that state, and federal documents go to the U.S. Department of State.

How much does a Illinois apostille cost?

The official page listed: $2 per apostille or certificate. Each document needs its own (last checked November 13, 2026). Confirm before paying, since fees change.

How long does a Illinois apostille take?

The official source listed: Mail requests: 7–14 business days (last checked November 13, 2026). Times change with volume.

How do I submit an apostille request in Illinois?

Mail or in person (Chicago office, select DMV+ locations)
