UCC filings and other common state filings

A UCC financing statement (often called a UCC-1) is a public notice that a lender has a security interest in a borrower's property, such as business equipment or inventory. In most states it's filed with, and searchable at, the Secretary of State.

UCC basics

  • UCC-1: filed by the secured party (usually the lender) to give public notice.
  • UCC-3: amends, continues, or terminates an earlier filing.
  • Searches: many states offer a UCC search on the same site used for business filings.
  • Some filings tied to real estate (fixture filings) are recorded at the county level instead.

Other filings you may run into

  • Trademarks (state level): many Secretaries of State register state trademarks. Federal trademarks are handled by the USPTO.
  • Assumed or fictitious business names (DBAs): filed with the state or the county, depending on the state.
  • Notary commissions: issued by the state, usually the Secretary of State or Lieutenant Governor.
  • Liens and judgments: often recorded with county offices or courts.

Where to go

Use your state's business filing office from the business filings directory, or open your state page.
